# Shotbase — Detailed Research

## Executive read

Shotbase positions itself as a complete capture workspace rather than a screenshot utility: **Capture → Edit → Animate → Organize → Share**. Its differentiators are local AI, an integrated visual library, web capture, animated/3D treatment, and a polished onboarding/brand experience.

The public site exposes a 7-day free trial with no card, Pro Yearly at **$9.75/month billed yearly** after a promotional reduction from $13, and Pro Monthly at **$18.75/month** after a promotional reduction from $25. Treat those as observed promotional prices, not permanent list prices.

## Capture

### Screenshots

- Capture an area, window, or full screen.
- Fast “tap to capture” entry point.
- The site implies a capture tray/history after capture.
- Screenshots can flow into the unified library.
- Quick annotations are listed in the plan feature inventory.

### Screen recording

- Record the screen as a video.
- Automatic zooms bring important moments closer.
- “Turn flat recordings into cinematic 3D” implies animated perspective/depth treatment.
- Finish recordings with text, shadow, and effects.
- The product groups recording editing into the same editor rather than requiring a separate editor.

### Web capture

- Capture a full webpage or only the viewport.
- Capture at mobile, tablet, desktop, or custom breakpoint sizes.
- Choose light or dark mode for the page capture.
- Requires a URL; terms say web-page capture depends on providing a URL and may send material to a service.
- Znip must make the capture boundary and privacy behavior explicit: local browser rendering vs remote fetch, authentication/cookies, robots, and dynamic content.

## Editor

Shotbase markets one editor for static and motion output:

- Backgrounds.
- Frames.
- Shadows.
- Borders.
- Watermarks.
- Arrows.
- Text.
- Highlights.
- Shapes.
- Callouts.
- Automatic zooms for walkthroughs.
- Presence/camera as the story anchor.
- 3D depth, movement, and perspective.
- Quick annotations.
- Custom backgrounds.
- Perfect padding.
- Social presets.
- Animated screenshots.

### Znip interpretation

The editor needs a non-destructive scene model. A screenshot and a recording should share primitives where possible: canvas, background, border, shadow, text, vector annotations, blur/pixelation, cursor, camera bubble, zoom keyframes, and export presets. Do not create a one-off screenshot editor and a disconnected video editor.

## Organize

- One library for screenshots, recordings, and webpage captures.
- Automatically generated useful filenames (“smart names”).
- Automatic tags based on content.
- Quick summaries.
- Fast search over names/tags.
- Visual library.
- AI labelling.
- Capture reminders.
- Capture tray history.

## Local AI

Shotbase explicitly claims that names, tags, and summaries are generated by AI running locally on the Mac; image content is not sent to a remote model for this feature. The site says local AI is available only on Apple-silicon Macs.

### Znip opportunity

- Preserve the local-first promise on Intel Macs by offering a graceful no-AI path and optional smaller models.
- Let users see and control each AI operation.
- Provide “never analyze this folder/tag” and “delete derived metadata.”
- Never send screenshot pixels, OCR text, filenames, private URLs, or notes to analytics.
- Separate local AI from optional cloud features in the permission model.

## Share

- Right-click a screenshot or recording in the library and upload it.
- Create a clean link in a few clicks.
- Shared links are viewable by recipients without Shotbase, according to the FAQ phrasing.
- Terms and privacy describe public links, title/description, view counts/reactions, and selected media upload.
- Sharing is opt-in; local captures are not automatically uploaded.

## Privacy and service model

Shotbase describes itself as local-first: captures, recordings, project files, and editing state generally stay on-device unless a cloud feature is chosen. Cloud processing/upload triggers include share-link creation, support attachments, and web-page capture. It uses account/authentication, a payment provider, analytics and cloud services for the service layer.

## Release intelligence

The public changelog shows a fast early cadence: versions 1.0.0, 1.0.1, 1.0.11, 1.1.0, 1.2.0, 1.3.x, and 1.4.x. Headline improvements include:

- Smaller, faster, smoother.
- Share instantly.
- Quieter account checks.
- More reliable recording.
- Safer updates.
- Smoother sign-in.
- Up to three devices.
- Smoother camera motion.
- Faster capture workflows.
- Reliable account sync.
- Easier account recovery.

These release names indicate that onboarding, permissions, recording reliability, device limits, update safety, and account friction are first-class product concerns—not polish to postpone.
